BRICS Summit 2026 New Delhi: What the Trade Agenda Means for Indian Exporters

BuyGenix Solutions Team · 7 min read

India hosted the 18th BRICS Leaders' Summit in New Delhi on 12-13 September 2026 — a 20th-anniversary gathering at Bharat Mandapam that brought together leaders from all 11 BRICS member nations. Beyond the geopolitical headlines, the summit's trade agenda carries real, practical implications for Indian exporters.

Why This Summit Mattered for Trade

India assumed its fourth BRICS chairship on 1 January 2026, choosing "Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability" as its theme. Over the course of the year, more than 350 ministerial and working-level meetings were held across 25 Indian cities — including a dedicated BRICS Trade Ministers' Meeting in Jaipur in August 2026 — building toward this leaders' summit. According to India's Commerce Ministry, intra-BRICS merchandise trade has grown from roughly $84 billion in 2003 to $1.17 trillion in 2024 — a thirteen-fold expansion — and BRICS nations now export about $1.1 trillion to one another, equal to nearly 19% of their combined global exports.

Who's in BRICS Now

The bloc has expanded well beyond its founding four members. BRICS today comprises Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa, Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Indonesia — 11 major emerging economies representing a substantial share of the world's population and economic output.

Key Trade Themes From the Summit

  • Cross-border digital payments — India pushed discussions on extending UPI-style digital payment interoperability across BRICS nations, aiming to make cross-border trade settlement faster and cheaper
  • Local currency trade — continued exploration of settling more bilateral trade in local currencies rather than relying solely on the US dollar
  • Logistics Supply-Chain Cooperation Framework — a new framework developed during India's chairship, focused on strengthening transport connectivity and more resilient, sustainable freight systems across member countries
  • Global Value Chains Action Plan (2026-2030) — a roadmap aimed at making supply chains among BRICS nations more resilient and productive
  • Trade finance access for MSMEs — proposals to make it easier for smaller exporters to access trade finance based more on cash flow than collateral

What Industry Bodies Are Saying

Industry groups have been vocal about what genuine success should look like. PHDCCI noted that "the success of BRICS should finally be measured by whether it makes cross-border trade and investment cheaper and simpler," while trade research body GTRI recommended that India push for better market access in China, Russia, and Indonesia specifically, address non-tariff barriers, and reduce excessive dependence on a small number of BRICS trading partners.

What This Means for Your Export Business

Even before any new framework is fully implemented, the summit signals a clear direction: BRICS trade is being actively engineered to become cheaper and less friction-heavy for exporters, particularly MSMEs. If your business already trades with — or is considering trading with — China, Russia, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Indonesia, Brazil, or other BRICS members, the coming implementation of the Global Value Chains Action Plan and Logistics Cooperation Framework are worth tracking closely over the next few years, since they're specifically designed to simplify customs, documentation, and supply-chain friction between these markets.
