Export Pricing: How to Calculate Your EXW, FOB and CIF Price

BuyGenix Solutions Team · 8 min read
Last updated 12 October 2026

Many new exporters take their domestic price, convert it to dollars and hope for the best. That approach either leaves money on the table or loses the order. A clear costing sheet shows you exactly what each Incoterm costs you and how low you can go before you lose money.

Quick answer

Start from your ex-works cost (product plus export packing), then add costs in order. EXW plus inland transport, port and customs broker charges gives the FOB cost. FOB plus ocean freight gives CFR, and CFR plus insurance gives CIF. Add your margin, deduct the export benefits you expect to receive, such as RoDTEP or duty drawback, and convert at a realistic exchange rate.

Step 1: Your ex-works cost

Begin with the full cost of the product: raw material, labour, overheads and the export packing the buyer expects, such as stronger cartons, pallets or shrink-wrap. Add any testing, inspection or certification the order needs. This is your EXW cost. GST is not part of this figure, because exports are zero-rated.

Step 2: Add costs up to FOB

  • Inland transport from your factory to the port, ICD or CFS
  • Container stuffing or CFS charges
  • Terminal handling charges at the port of loading
  • Customs broker fees and documentation charges
  • Certificate of origin, phytosanitary or other certificates if needed

Step 3: Add freight and insurance for CFR and CIF

For a CFR price, add the ocean freight quoted by your freight forwarder to the destination port. For CIF, add marine insurance, normally calculated on 110% of the CIF value. Freight rates change often, so put a validity date on any CFR or CIF quotation.

These figures are only an illustration. Add your margin to each level to get your selling price, then divide by the quantity for a unit price.

Margin, export benefits and currency

Decide your margin on each level separately. Then factor in what you expect to recover after shipment, such as RoDTEP, duty drawback or a GST refund on inputs, which can let you price more competitively. Finally, convert at a cautious exchange rate rather than today's best rate, because the rupee can move between quotation and payment. If you quote in dollars, the currency risk is yours until payment arrives.

Frequently asked questions

Is GST included in the export price?

No. Exports are zero-rated, so GST is not added to the export price. You either export under LUT without paying IGST, or pay IGST and claim a refund.

Should I quote FOB or CIF?

Quote FOB if the buyer has its own freight agent. Quote CIF or CFR if the buyer wants a landed-port price or you can get better freight rates.

How do I handle currency changes in my price?

Use a cautious exchange rate, keep quotations valid for a short period, and consider forward contracts with your bank for large orders.

Should export benefits be included in the price?

Many exporters factor in the RoDTEP or drawback they expect to receive so they can price more competitively, but only count benefits you are sure to get.
