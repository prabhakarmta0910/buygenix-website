A registration, like GST or IEC, generally confirms your business exists and is authorised to operate in a certain way. A licence, like an FSSAI licence or an export authorisation, is regulatory permission tied to a specific activity, and often comes with ongoing compliance obligations.

GST Registration generally takes 3 to 7 working days, depending on document verification and government approval.

Yes. Unlike domestic businesses, exporters must register for GST regardless of turnover, since exports are treated as inter-state supply under Indian tax law.

A Letter of Undertaking (LUT) lets you export without paying IGST upfront. It's valid for one financial year and must be renewed annually to keep exporting duty-free.

Exporters can claim a refund of accumulated Input Tax Credit through the GST portal, even with an LUT in place — timely filing keeps your working capital from getting tied up.

An IEC (Import Export Code) is a mandatory 10-digit registration for anyone importing or exporting from India. It's typically issued within 1 to 3 working days once documents are submitted correctly.

The IEC itself doesn't expire, but DGFT requires you to confirm and update your IEC details annually between April and June — skipping this can get your IEC deactivated.

RCMC (Registration-Cum-Membership Certificate) confirms your membership with the Export Promotion Council relevant to your product — for example APEDA for food products or EEPC for engineering goods. We help you identify the right council and register.

MOOWR lets manufacturers import raw materials and capital goods into a bonded warehouse without paying duty upfront. It's worth considering if you're import-heavy and want to improve working capital without committing to export-only operations.

Yes — any business exporting food products needs a Central FSSAI licence, regardless of turnover, simply because export activity triggers this requirement.

An AD Code links your bank branch to your export forex transactions and is mandatory for customs clearance. It has to be registered separately at every port you ship from — one port's registration doesn't cover another.
