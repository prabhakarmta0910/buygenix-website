FDA Registration & Prior Notice for Exporting Food to the USA: Complete Guide

BuyGenix Solutions Team · 7 min read

Spices, snacks, packaged foods, dietary supplements — if it's food and it's headed to the United States, it can't clear the border on GST registration and an IEC code alone. The FDA has its own separate compliance layer, and missing it is one of the fastest ways to get a shipment detained at a US port.

Who Needs FDA Registration

Under the Bioterrorism Act and the Food Safety Modernization Act (FSMA), any foreign facility that manufactures, processes, packs, or holds food for human or animal consumption in the US must register with the FDA before exporting — regardless of company size or shipment volume. This covers spices, packaged snacks, dietary supplements, beverages, and processed agricultural products alike.

The U.S. Agent Requirement

Every foreign food facility registering with the FDA must designate a U.S. Agent — a person or company physically present in the United States who serves as the FDA's point of contact for that facility. The U.S. Agent isn't optional paperwork; the FDA communicates directly with this agent for inspections, questions, and any compliance issues, so choosing a responsive, knowledgeable agent matters as much as the registration itself.

Prior Notice: A Separate Filing for Every Single Shipment

Facility registration is a one-time (renewable) step. Prior Notice is required separately for every shipment and must reach the FDA within a strict window before arrival:

  • By road: at least 2 hours before arrival
  • By rail or air: at least 4 hours before arrival
  • By water (ocean freight): at least 8 hours before arrival
  • By international mail or courier: before the package is even shipped

Prior Notice can be filed up to 15 days in advance through the FDA's PNSI system, or up to 30 days in advance through ABI/ACS via a customs broker. A missing or late Prior Notice is grounds for the FDA to refuse entry outright — a completely avoidable delay that catches first-time exporters off guard more often than any other single requirement.

Registration Renewal — Don't Let It Lapse

FDA food facility registrations must be renewed periodically (currently on a biennial cycle), and a lapsed registration blocks shipments just as effectively as never having registered at all. Exporters juggling multiple compliance deadlines across GST, FSSAI, and FDA sometimes lose track of the FDA renewal specifically, since it's a US-side deadline rather than an Indian one.
