IEC Code Registration in India: Why You Can't Export Without It

BuyGenix Solutions Team · 5 min read

No matter how strong your product or how ready your buyer is to place an order, your shipment cannot legally clear Indian customs without one specific registration: the Import Export Code (IEC). Here's exactly what it is, who needs it, and how to get one.

What is an IEC Code and Who Needs One

An IEC is a 10-digit registration number issued by the Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) that's mandatory for any business or individual looking to import or export goods and services from India. Without it, banks won't process your international payments for trade, and customs authorities won't clear your shipments. Any proprietorship, partnership, LLP, or company planning to sell products internationally needs to register for an IEC before their first shipment.

Documents Required for IEC Code Registration

  • PAN card of the business or individual applicant
  • Aadhaar card or passport of the proprietor/authorized signatory
  • Bank account details along with a cancelled cheque or bank certificate
  • Address proof of the business premises
  • Digital signature (for company/LLP applicants, in some cases)

Step-by-Step IEC Code Application Process

  • Step 1: Register on the DGFT portal using your PAN and business details.
  • Step 2: Fill out the online IEC application form (ANF-2A) with business and bank details.
  • Step 3: Upload the required documents in the specified format.
  • Step 4: Pay the applicable government fee online.
  • Step 5: Once verified, the IEC certificate is issued digitally — typically within 1–3 working days.

Is IEC Code Renewal Required Every Year

The IEC itself does not expire and does not need to be renewed annually. However, DGFT requires all IEC holders to confirm and update their IEC details every year between April and June, even if there are no changes. Failing to do this update can result in the IEC being deactivated, which would block your ability to export until it's reactivated — so this is one compliance date every exporter should mark on the calendar.

Getting your IEC code right the first time — and staying on top of the annual confirmation — keeps your export operations running without last-minute customs or banking delays.
