Importing from India to France: Trade Compliance Guide for France Buyers

BuyGenix Solutions Team · 6 min read

If your business in France is exploring India as a sourcing destination, the products are usually the easy part — it's the customs, documentation, and compliance side that trips up first-time importers. Here's what France buyers actually need to know before placing that first order.

Why France Buyers Are Looking to India

France is a major European market for Indian textiles, leather goods, and processed agricultural products, with growing interest in Indian engineering and pharmaceutical exports too. For France businesses looking to diversify suppliers, reduce costs, or access categories India specialises in, working directly with Indian exporters — rather than through multiple layers of trading intermediaries — is increasingly the more competitive option.

Customs & Duty Considerations

Imports from India into France are cleared through French Customs (Douane), operating under the EU Customs Union. as an EU member state, France will benefit from the EU-India Free Trade Agreement once it's ratified, following the conclusion of negotiations in January 2026 Before finalising pricing with an Indian supplier, confirm the exact duty treatment for your product's specific HS code — assuming a general rate based on the product category alone is one of the most common, and costly, mistakes new importers make.

Documents You Should Expect From a Compliant Indian Exporter

A genuinely compliant Indian exporter will readily provide a valid IEC (Import Export Code), GST registration details, a commercial invoice matching your purchase order exactly, a packing list, and a Certificate of Origin where relevant to your trade terms. If a supplier is reluctant to share these, or the documents don't match what they've verbally quoted, treat that as a warning sign rather than a minor inconsistency.

Common Product Categories France Imports from India

France businesses most commonly import textiles and apparel, leather goods, gems and jewellery, processed foods, and chemicals from India, though sourcing patterns continue to diversify as Indian manufacturing capacity expands across engineering, electronics, and specialty chemicals as well.
