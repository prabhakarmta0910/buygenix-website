How to Import from India to the UK in 2026: CETA Duties, VAT & Documents

BuyGenix Solutions Team · 11 min read
Last updated 2 October 2026For UK importers, buyers and sourcing teams
Quick answer

To import from India to the UK you need a GB EORI number, the correct UK commodity code for your product, and a customs declaration on HMRC's Customs Declaration Service (CDS), usually filed by a customs agent. Since the India-UK CETA came into force on 15 July 2026, most Indian goods can enter the UK at zero or reduced duty if the exporter supplies valid proof of origin. Import VAT, normally 20%, still applies, but VAT-registered businesses can defer it with postponed VAT accounting.

Step-by-step: how to import from India to the UK

  1. Get a GB EORI numberEvery UK business that imports needs an Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number starting with GB. It is free and you apply on GOV.UK.
  2. Find the commodity codeClassify your product in the UK Integrated Online Tariff. The 10-digit code decides the duty rate, the CETA preference available and any licences or checks.
  3. Check the duty and any controlsLook up the third-country rate and the preferential rate for India, and whether your product needs a licence, health certificate or pre-notification.
  4. Choose a compliant Indian supplierConfirm the supplier's IEC and GST registration, and agree specifications, Incoterms, payment terms and who prepares the origin declaration.
  5. Agree the proof of originTo claim CETA rates, the exporter provides an origin declaration on the commercial invoice or a certificate of origin. Check the wording before the goods ship.
  6. Appoint a customs agent or freight forwarderMost UK importers use an agent to file the CDS import declaration, claim the preference and arrange delivery from the port or airport.
  7. Pay or defer duty and import VATPay through a duty deferment account or at clearance, and account for import VAT on your VAT return if you use postponed VAT accounting.
  8. Keep your recordsKeep the declaration, invoice, origin proof and transport documents. HMRC can check origin claims after import.

Customs duty under the India-UK CETA

The UK-India Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA) was signed on 24 July 2025 and came into force on 15 July 2026. Around 99% of Indian tariff lines can now enter the UK duty-free, covering major categories such as textiles and apparel, leather and footwear, gems and jewellery, marine products, processed food, engineering goods and chemicals. A small number of sensitive products keep longer phase-in periods or exclusions, so always check your exact commodity code.

The preferential rate is not automatic. You only get it when:

  • the goods meet the CETA rules of origin: wholly obtained in India, made entirely from originating materials, or sufficiently processed in India under the product-specific rule;
  • you hold valid proof of origin; and
  • your customs declaration on CDS uses the correct preference code and country of origin and references the proof of origin.
Proof of originWho provides itWhen it is used
Origin declarationThe Indian exporter or producer, self-certified on the invoice or another commercial documentThe usual route for most shipments
Certificate of originIssued in India on the exporter's applicationWhen the exporter prefers or the buyer asks for a certificate
Importer's knowledgeYou, the UK importer, based on your own information about how the goods were madeLimited cases where you hold the evidence yourself
If the paperwork is missing or wrong, the goods still clear, but at the full UK Global Tariff rate. Check the origin declaration with your supplier before shipment, not after the goods land.

Import VAT and postponed VAT accounting

CETA removes or reduces customs duty, not VAT. Import VAT is charged on the customs value plus duty and transport costs to the UK, usually at the standard rate of 20%, with reduced or zero rates for some goods. If you are VAT-registered, postponed VAT accounting lets you declare and reclaim import VAT on the same VAT return instead of paying it at the border, which protects your cash flow.

Shipping, Incoterms and payment

  • Sea freight from ports such as Nhava Sheva (Mumbai), Mundra and Chennai to Felixstowe, Southampton or London Gateway usually takes several weeks door to door. Air freight takes a few days and suits samples and high-value goods.
  • Incoterms: FOB and CIF are common for sea freight; agree who pays freight, insurance and UK clearance before you confirm the order.
  • Payment: new buyer-supplier relationships often start with a letter of credit or part-advance payment, moving to open account once trust is built.
  • Samples and inspection: approve pre-production samples and use a pre-shipment inspection for first orders.

Mistakes that cost UK importers money

  • Using a commodity code based on the product category instead of the exact product, so the duty or preference claimed is wrong
  • Shipping without a valid origin declaration and paying full duty that CETA would have removed
  • Assuming CETA also removes import VAT
  • Not checking product rules such as labelling, UKCA marking or a UK Responsible Person before the goods arrive
  • Working with suppliers who cannot show an IEC and GST registration, or whose documents do not match the quotation
  • Not keeping origin and import records for HMRC checks

Frequently asked questions

Is importing from India to the UK duty-free now?

For most products, yes, if the goods meet the CETA rules of origin and you have valid proof of origin. Around 99% of Indian tariff lines can enter duty-free, but some sensitive products are phased in or excluded, so check your commodity code.

Do I still pay VAT on imports from India?

Yes. CETA covers customs duty, not VAT. Import VAT is usually 20%, and VAT-registered businesses can use postponed VAT accounting.

What do I need to start importing from India?

A GB EORI number, the correct commodity code for your product, a compliant Indian supplier, proof of origin to claim CETA rates, and a customs agent or your own access to file the CDS declaration.

Who provides the proof of origin?

Usually the Indian exporter, through a self-certified origin declaration on the invoice. A certificate of origin issued in India, or importer's knowledge in limited cases, can also be used.

How long does shipping from India to the UK take?

Sea freight usually takes several weeks door to door, depending on the ports and route. Air freight takes a few days.
