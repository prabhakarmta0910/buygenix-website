What goes on an export proforma invoice

A proforma invoice is the quotation your buyer uses to confirm an order, open a letter of credit or apply for an import licence. If it is complete and matches what you will ship, the commercial invoice and the rest of the documents follow without changes.

FieldWhy it matters
HS codeLets the buyer check import duty and any licence before confirming. Use the code for the exact product, not the category.
Incoterm and named placeDecides who pays freight and insurance and where risk passes, for example FOB Nhava Sheva or CIF Jebel Ali.
Payment termsAdvance, letter of credit, D/P or D/A. Banks follow these words, so write them exactly.
ValidityFreight rates and exchange rates move. A validity date protects your price.
Bank detailsAccount name, number and SWIFT code for the advance or LC proceeds.

Frequently asked questions

What is a proforma invoice in export?

A proforma invoice is a quotation in invoice format that an exporter sends before shipment. It lists the goods, quantities, prices, Incoterm, payment terms and delivery time, so the buyer can confirm the order, open a letter of credit or arrange an import licence.

Is a proforma invoice the same as a commercial invoice?

No. A proforma invoice is sent before the sale is final and is not a demand for payment. The commercial invoice is issued at shipment and is the document customs uses for valuation.

What should an export proforma invoice include?

Exporter and buyer details, invoice number and date, a description of each product with HS code, quantity, unit price and total, the currency, the Incoterm and named place, ports of loading and discharge, country of origin, payment terms, delivery time, validity of the offer and bank details.

Can I use a proforma invoice for advance payment?

Yes. Buyers often pay an advance against a proforma invoice. Keep the bank details accurate and state the advance percentage in the payment terms.
